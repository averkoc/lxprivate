Drum Pattern Guide & Reference

Core Principles & Elements

Kick (Bass Drum)

Foundation of the groove, defines pulse and weight. Low frequencies feel grounding; beats 1 and 3 are natural anchors.

Snare

Backbeat and contrast to the kick. Placed typically on beats 2 & 4. Feels like natural "clap" points in rock/pop.

Hi-Hats (Closed & Open)

Timekeeping and texture. Closed hats define divisions (8ths, 16ths). Open hats emphasize off-beats or transitions.

Piano Bass & Piano Chord

Two pitched (non-percussion) tracks for patterns like Stride that aren't drum-kit rhythms at all. Piano Bass plays a low root note that automatically shifts up a fifth in bar 2 — the standard stride alternation. Piano Chord plays a fixed triad. Both export to their own melodic MIDI channel with a proper instrument program, separate from the drum channel.

Bars

Most grooves repeat every bar, but some — like Stride's root/fifth alternation, or a 2-bar clave/turnaround — only reveal their full shape across two bars. Use the Bars control to switch the grid between a 1-bar and 2-bar loop.

Common Rhythmic Forms

Rock Backbeat

Kick:  1--- ---- 1--- ----
Snare: ---- 2--- ---- 4---
Hats:  x-x- x-x- x-x- x-x-

Pop / EDM (Four-on-the-Floor)

Kick:  1--- 2--- 3--- 4---
Snare: ---- ---- ---- ----
Hats:  x-x- x-x- x-x- x-x-

Hip-Hop (Boom-Bap)

Kick:  1--- ---- --3- ----
Snare: ---- 2--- ---- 4---
Hats:  x-x- x-x- x-x- x-x-

Funk Pocket

Kick:  1--e ---- --3- ----
Snare: ---- 2--- ---- 4---
Hats:  xxxx xxxx xxxx xxxx

Reggae (One-Drop)

Kick:  ---- ---- 3--- ----
Snare: ---- ---- 3--- ----
Hats:  -x-x -x-x -x-x -x-x

Dembow / Reggaeton

Kick:  1--- ---- --3- ----
Snare: ---- -2-- ---- 4---
Hats:  x-x- x-x- x-x- x-x-

Charleston (4/4)

Kick:  1--- --&--- ---- ----
Hats:  x-x- x-x- x-x- x-x-

Two accents: beat 1 and the "and" of beat 2. Historically a comping figure, not a literal drum pattern — shown here as kick + hi-hat accents.

Boogie-Woogie (4/4, swung)

Kick:  x-x- x-x- x-x- x-x-  (8-to-the-bar)
Snare: ---- 2--- ---- 4---

Set Swing to ~60% for the triplet-based shuffle feel underneath the steady 8th-note pulse.

Swing / Shuffle (4/4, swung)

Hats:  1--- -&-- 3--- -&--  (ride/hat approximation)
Kick:  1--- ---- 3--- ----

Approximates the jazz "spang-a-lang" ride pattern in a 16-step grid. Set Swing to ~65% to push the off-beats toward a triplet feel.

12/8 Triplet Feel

Kick:  1-- 2-- 3-- 4--
Snare: ---- x -- ---- x --
Hats:  xxx xxx xxx xxx

4 dotted-quarter beats, each naturally divided into 3 (true triplets, not simulated swing). Common in blues ballads and slow blues shuffles.

Stride Piano (2 bars)

Bar 1 — Bass: 1--- ---- 3--- ----  (root)
       Chord:---- 2--- ---- 4---
Bar 2 — Bass: 1--- ---- 3--- ----  (fifth)
       Chord:---- 2--- ---- 4---

The left hand's classic "oom-pah": a low bass note on beats 1 & 3, a mid-register chord on 2 & 4. The bass note alternates — typically root, then fifth — every other bar, which is why this preset spans 2 bars instead of 1: a single bar can't show the alternation, only one half of it. Uses the new Piano Bass / Piano Chord tracks (real pitched notes, not GM percussion), so it exports to MuseScore on its own instrument staff rather than the drum staff.

Optimizing for Headsets vs. Phone Speakers

Phones cannot output true low frequencies below 150 Hz, which causes kick drum "thump" to disappear. To make loops punchy across both setups, try layering mid/high transient accents on your downbeats inside MuseScore:

  • Kanttilyönti (Rimshot): Adds a sharp 2–5 kHz transient spike.
  • Matala lattiatomi (Low Floor Tom): Reinforces mid-bass frequencies (120–250 Hz).
  • Lehmänkello / Tamburiini: Clear mid-range and high markers that cut through small speakers seamlessly.
